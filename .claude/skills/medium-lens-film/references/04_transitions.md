# 04 — Transition catalogue (frame-accurate, 30 fps)

Every transition in 《光先到》, read frame by frame. Durations are in frames (1 f = 33 ms). The main
lesson: **a transition is made of the physics of one of the two media**, and the best ones are
**content-aligned** (a feature of the outgoing image becomes a feature of the incoming one).

## Patterns to reuse

1. **Accelerate the material.** Early transitions are slow and physical (7–12 f: cracking, tearing,
   bleeding, printing). The middle ones are fast (3–4 f: glass, burns, paint). The late ones are
   near-instant (1–3 f: cards, static, flashes). Transition speed tracks the montage's acceleration.
2. **Content-aligned hand-offs** (T1, T5, T7, T9, T23): place the incoming medium's key feature exactly
   where the outgoing image's bright feature is. The eye follows the brightest point across the cut.
3. **Single-frame accents** (T10 subliminal pop grid, T13 speed-line frame, T18 black frame with a dot,
   T27 collapsed column): one frame, felt more than seen. They add energy at no cost.
4. **Media as physical cards** (T16–T20): once the audience has learned the medium = object idea,
   the objects can fly past the camera. Motion blur sells the speed.
5. **Glass cracks recur** (T14, T17, T19, T20) as a unifying "break the medium" gesture.
6. **White frames** separate chapters (the flash, 9.83, 10.85, 14.2) and echo the explosion itself.
7. **Match-cut by scale** (T23–T26): the camera moves through nested frames (cloud → pupil → eye →
   window → building → window → room), turning the ending into one continuous pull-out and rush-in.
